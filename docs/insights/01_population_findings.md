## Population findings (all 4,500 accounts)

"At risk" means under 3 months of runway after a job loss with EI at 55% (10% of accounts, 454).

What is associated with running out of money:
- A low savings rate is the strongest signal. 30% of accounts in the lowest savings-rate quartile (under about 5%) are at risk, against 0.2% in the top quartile.
- Frequent deficit months: the share of months with spending above income is strongly tied to short runway.
- Cash reliance: accounts whose income is almost all cash deposits are at risk 26% of the time (median runway 4.2 months, against 28.7 months with no cash deposits). Accounts that spend over two thirds of outflows as cash withdrawals are at risk 14-16% of the time, against 3% under one half.
- Volatile income: the most volatile quarter is at risk 17% of the time.
- Loans: loan holders are at risk 22% of the time (8% without). Loans in arrears or default go with very thin buffers (about 1-1.5 months of spending). When the payment is a large share of income, default is more common (23% in the top quarter).
- Demographics, region and district unemployment show little or no effect. Pensioners are almost never at risk because pension income continues.

What helps:
- At-risk accounts send a median 74% of outflows to cash withdrawals, the main flexible category in this data. Cutting cash withdrawals 10% lifts 35% of at-risk accounts above 3 months of runway; 20% lifts 58%; 30% lifts 75%.
- 19% of accounts are below a 3-month buffer and have no monthly surplus, so saving alone cannot help them without a spending change.

Caution: these are associations in partly synthetic data, not causes.
